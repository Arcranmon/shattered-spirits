
### Making Camp
After a long day of travel, the party will typically make camp. When they do, the party has a chance to decompress, gather themselves, and rest. The party must rest for at least two segments of the day, performing no activities, or else suffer harsh penalties. Any other segments of the day not used for either traveling or resting may be used for _Camp Actions_.

&nbsp;

Typically, the party will choose to make camp when _Time Passes_ on a card from the _Travel Deck_. If they do not, set aside the top of the _Travel Deck_ until the next _Time Passes_ card, and place it at the top of the next day's _Travel Deck_.

&nbsp;

### Camp Quality
Campsites have three base values that determine their general quality: Comfort, Security, and Concealment. These values are all tracked from -6 to 6, providing various bonuses while positive and detriments while negative. Additionally, campsites might have additional traits that modify these values or provide some other benefit.

&nbsp;

**Comfort** covers how well a party can rest, and is typically increased with quality food, a warm fire, and a comfortable space to lie down. Campsites with a high Comfort will allow the party to better recover and potentially clear some _Stress_, while low Comfort campsites can make any sort of recovery almost impossible.

&nbsp;

**Security** is a measure of how well fortified, how easy to see approaching creatures, and how wary the party is. While there is a high security score enemies will be less likely to approach the party and, if they do, the party will have advanced warning and an advantageous position. If it is low, creatures that would otherwise leave the party alone may attempt to take advantage of the situation. 

&nbsp;

Whenever an encounter is drawn at camp, it will have a _Security_ treshold.
* If the camp's _Security_ exceeds it by 3 the encounter is noticed, but does not attempt to threaten the party.
* If the camp's _Security_ exceeds it by 2 they notice well in advance and can respond accordingly.
* If the camp's _Security_ exceeds it by 1 they have some warning and can respond accordingly.
* If the camp's _Security_ meets the score the party is not surprised and all party members that did not _Crash_ can respond.
* If the camp's _Security_ is less than the score the encounter surprises them, and they either take a penalty on rolls of begin the combat _Surprised_.
* If the camp's _Security_ is 2 or more less the party is badly surprised and the encounter becomes worse in some way.


In addition to their respective _Comfort_, _Security_, and _Concealment_ scores, campsites also might have assorted other tags that supply other effects. For example, a Covered campsite might mitigate some of the penalties caused by weather or other concerns.

&nbsp;

**Concealment** is how difficult to find the party's camp is. A camp with low concealment is more likely to have encounters and said encounters are more likely to occur.

&nbsp;

Whenever an encounter is drawn at camp, it will have a _Concealment_ threshold. If the camp's _Concealment_ meets or exceeds this threshold, the encounter does not take place. If the camp's _Concealment_ is less than the threshold, the encounter happens unless the camp's _Security_ score would prevent it. If an encounter occurs due to the party being tracked, subtract the number of characters that did not cover their tracks from the camp's _Concealment_ score.

&nbsp;

For any random encounters, for every 2 points below 0 the camp's _Concealment_ is add an encounter. For every 3 points above 0, remove an encounter.

### Camp Actions

After finishing their Camp Action, characters will mark off _Supplies_ to avoid gaining _Hungry_ and additional _Supplies_ to avoid gaining _Thirsty_. Some effects allow characters to consume additional _Supplies_ for some effect, but each effect should only be accomplished once. 

&nbsp;

Additionally, when breaking camp, characters must decide if they wish to make a fire. Doing so grants +1 _Comfort_ and _Security_ and allows the party to cook, but causes -2 _Concealment_ and costs _Supplies_.

&nbsp;
### The Rest Roll

Characters and spirits then make a **Rest Roll** to see how well they're able to recover. The _Rest Roll_ typically has an automatic Stake of a single **Efficacy**, and often includes the following:

&nbsp;
**Dangers**
* **Affliction (Crash):** You sleep far too heavily or are otherwise unaware, leaving you unresponsive to any dangers for the night. If at least half the party  does not remove this _Danger_, decrease the _Security_ of the camp by 2.

&nbsp;
**Augments**
* **Efficacy:** Mark a segment on a _Condition_ clock or, if this environment is not hostile to your craft, regain 1 _Craft_. Spirits may instead choose to restore a _Durability_ to any _Equipment_ granted to them by their _Traits_.

&nbsp;

Characters always roll their _Rest Roll_ _Cautiously_, unless specified otherwise. Characters must rest for a full two segments; for each segment they do not rest for, they automatically gain a _Fatigue_ at the end of the rest. If they don't rest at all, they do not make a _Rest Roll_.

&nbsp;

In a place of power characters may gain an automatic _Efficacy_ stake that can only be used to restore _Craft_.
